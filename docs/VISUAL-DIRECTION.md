# Visual Direction

## Editorial approach

Narrative-first data journalism. The reader should encounter a compelling headline and explanation before being asked to interpret a chart. Use a deliberate sequence of chapters to build the argument.

## Visual language

- Strong, claim-led headlines and readable editorial typography
- Focused explanatory charts rather than collections of KPI cards
- Annotations that point to meaningful changes or comparisons
- Clear chapter markers and transitions
- Source notes and caveats adjacent to the evidence
- A restrained, accessible palette with distinct colours for meaningful categories
- Whitespace and consistent alignment to support long-form reading

## Interaction and motion

Interactions should reveal context, let readers explore a comparison, or help them navigate chapters. Keep essential explanations visible without interaction. Use subtle transitions for chapter progression and respect reduced-motion preferences.

## Avoid

- Operational dashboard layouts, alert panels, and decorative KPI grids
- Charts without a clear narrative question
- Colour used only for decoration or status without labels
- Unsupported causal language or false precision
- Motion that interrupts reading or carries information unavailable elsewhere
- Official government branding or visual treatment that suggests endorsement

## Accessibility and responsive design

Use semantic headings, keyboard-operable controls, visible focus states, sufficient contrast, and text summaries for important graphics. Design chapter layouts for narrow screens first; charts must remain legible without horizontal page scrolling.
